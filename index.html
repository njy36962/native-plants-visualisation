01

Composition & recorded presence

Where does diversity stand out?

Start by comparing the official list of recognised native plant species used in this project with records of where and when those plants were found. Plants are organised into families, which are broad groups of related plants, and genera, which are smaller groups within a family. Explore these groups, then compare Australia’s states and territories. Look at both the number of different species recorded and the percentage nationally listed as vulnerable, endangered or critically endangered. These categories identify species assessed as being at risk of extinction.

A quick guide to Australia’s jurisdictions

Australia is southeast of Asia. These charts compare its six states and two main mainland territories. They exclude the separate Other Territories grouping and Jervis Bay. The ACT is a small territory within southeastern Australia.

02

Evidence & its limits

How much can records tell us?

The first section showed documented richness. Now examine the evidence behind it. An occurrence record documents a species at a place and time. The line chart shows when those records were made; the point map shows a sample of where. Both describe documentation, so more records can reflect more collecting, reporting or digitisation.

How to read the next map

Switch species. Compare the footprint.

Start with the default example, then choose another species. Does the displayed footprint span many jurisdictions or concentrate in a smaller area? Each equal-sized dot is a sampled record at its original published location.

The examples are selected by documented footprint and record availability. A narrow footprint in this subset does not prove the species lives nowhere else.

Only records with known coordinate uncertainty within the stated limit are displayed. Sensitive and generalised locations are omitted.

03

National conservation context

Which groups carry assessed risk?

Change the lens from recording effort to assessed conservation status. National listings identify a threatened subset of the flora. Vulnerable, endangered and critically endangered are increasingly severe categories. Here, extant means not listed as extinct. Begin with the critically endangered share, follow families into their categories, then compare the geographic patterns. Current listings are used even when an occurrence is older.

A careful denominator

Keep the listed set in view.

The waffle and flow diagram describe the matched extant nationally threatened set. They do not show the proportion of all Australian plants that is threatened.

Unmatched names and conflicting categories are excluded from status assignment. An unassigned species is not labelled “least concern”.

Extinct matches are excluded from this extant-species story.

Landscape context

How uneven is protected land?

The final question concerns land rather than species. How much terrestrial land is protected, and how does coverage vary? Start with ecological bioregions, which are areas with related landscape characteristics. Then compare states and territories. The histogram reveals a distribution that a single national percentage would hide. The scatterplot places land protection beside recorded threatened share, with their different denominators and dates kept explicit.

Behind the figures

Sources, scope & attribution

How the data were prepared

Accepted native scientific and phrase-name species were matched by exact taxon identifier. Records require native origin in their recorded jurisdiction, valid dates and coordinates, and an eligible licence. Duplicate record IDs count once. Hybrids, intergrades and known national Extinct matches are outside the selected checklist.

Species-level national matches are assigned only where the category is unambiguous. Sensitive/generalised locations and unknown uncertainty are omitted from the point display. Other charts use the full retained universe.

Read with these limits

Documented richness is influenced by recording effort and publication filters. Native origin describes a taxon, not proof that each record is wild. No match is not a safety assessment. State labels are used as supplied; display geometry is not used to calculate land area.

Occurrence documentation, current conservation listings and protection snapshots describe different times. Their comparison does not establish population change or conservation success.
